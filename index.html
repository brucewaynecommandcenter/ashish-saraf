Ashish Saraf

A global citizen and third-generation industrialist. Chairman of Open Origin India Inc and of Saraf Enterprises Pvt. Ltd., and the first ever Special Envoy of Antigua and Barbuda to India, Nepal and Sri Lanka.

A Non-Resident Indian based in London and New York, he draws on four decades of industrial experience to bring new technologies to India: artificial intelligence, defence technology, data centres, and the ammonia and hydrogen the country increasingly needs.

Biography

Born in Nagpur, Central India, in 1965. A Non-Resident Indian, he lives between New Delhi, Dubai, London and New York.

His paternal family business was founded by the late Shri Durga Prasad Saraf. His maternal grandfather, the late Rai Bahadur Gujar Mal Modi, founded the town of Modinagar in 1932.

Family

He is blessed with three children. His elder daughter, Aisha Saraf, a graduate of The Wharton School (2011), runs the fashion company AISPI. She is married to Ashin Kothari, a Carnegie Mellon graduate and diamond trader in Antwerp, and they have two daughters, Alaia and Alizey.

His second daughter, Avika Saraf, graduated from the University of California, Berkeley, earned her MBA at MIT Sloan, and works at Evercore Private Equity in New York. She is married to Dhruv Bathla, an MIT Sloan MBA in the health care sector.

His son, Devansh Saraf, is pursuing a career in film direction at NYU's Tisch School of the Arts.

    The Industrialist

    Today his work centres on Open Origin. His industrial roots lie in his family's enterprises: his paternal family, from Nagpur in Central India, pioneered the mining of manganese and chrome ore in the 1940s and became India's largest producer of ferro chrome and manganese. The Saraf Group has since grown into the world's third largest producer of polyester film, with plants in India, Thailand, Turkey and the USA.

    Open Origin

    Fuel the future, preserve the planet

    Today Ashish Saraf is Chairman of Open Origin India Inc, part of Open Origin Inc, a US company setting up a 5.8 billion dollar green ammonia production facility together with a 4,000 MW solar plant in New Mexico, USA.

    Through Open Origin he is working to meet India's growing need for ammonia and hydrogen, alongside new technologies in artificial intelligence, defence and data centres.

    How a facility works

    • 720,000 metric tons of green ammonia a year, about 2,000 tons a day
    • About 10,000 acres including on-site power: a 3,400 MW off-grid solar plant and a 2,700 MW AEM hydrogen plant
    • Only water, air and sunlight go in: no CO2, no emissions and no wastewater
    • Permit to full production in 24 to 30 months, at a cost competitive with grey ammonia
    • Built for non-arable land, with a modular site plan for expansion, and 1,000+ jobs created

    Where green ammonia is used

    • Zero-emission fuel for shipping and transport
    • Synthesis of high value chemicals and pharmaceuticals
    • Zero-emission electricity through fuel cells
    • Fertilizer for eco-friendly agriculture and more resilient crops

    Bringing it to India

    Open Origin has proposed a green ammonia facility to the Government of Maharashtra's Industries, Energy, Labour and Mining Department.

    • 720,000 metric tons a year
    • 1,000 new jobs
    • Foreign direct investment of about US$1.75 billion (Rs. 15,000 crore)

    The FACOR years

    Where his career began. From 1987 he worked in his family's FACOR Group, one of India's largest ferro alloys producers at about 140,000 tons a year, with chrome ore mines in Orissa, plants in Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra and Orissa, and exports to Asia, Europe, North America and beyond. He later served as its Joint Managing Director.

    The extended family enterprise

    His maternal grandfather founded Modinagar, 60 km from New Delhi, in 1932 to produce essential goods from sugar and steel to textiles and pharmaceuticals. The Modi family brought industrialisation to Northern India and many of India's landmark joint ventures, including Xerox, Disney, Philip Morris, Revlon, Alcatel, Olivetti and Lufthansa.

      These listed companies have a direct family connection with Ashish Saraf. Together they are worth more than US$10 billion and employ over 100,000 people in India. In his global role he offers international partners direct access to these industries and to India's market of 1.4 billion people.
